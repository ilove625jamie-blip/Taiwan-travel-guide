Before You Go

Visa, money, phone data, weather and plugs: everything to sort out before you fly.

Visa & entry

Indonesian passport holders need permission before entering Taiwan. There are two routes.

A. Online TAC

Travel Authorization Certificate. Free, online, tourism only. Valid 90 days; stay up to 30 days.

Who can apply (Bureau of Consular Affairs)

B. Visitor visa

If you do not meet the TAC conditions, apply for a visitor visa at the Taipei Economic and Trade Office in Jakarta, before you travel.

TETO Jakarta (official)
Bureau of Consular Affairs

TAC basic requirements

  • Passport valid for at least 6 months from arrival
  • A return or onward ticket (CI761, Dec 28)
  • Never worked as a blue-collar worker in Taiwan

And one of these

  • A valid visa or residence card (or one expired less than 10 years ago) from Australia, Canada, Japan, Korea, New Zealand, a Schengen country, the UK or the US
  • A Taiwan visa or residence card from the past 10 years with a clean record
  • A valid Japan Visa Waiver Registration
Apply earlyIf you are not eligible for the TAC, a visa takes time. Rules may have changed since the official page was last updated (2023), so confirm on the official sites above.

If required, also complete the online arrival card: arrival card.

Carry cashNight markets, small eateries and breakfast shops mostly take cash only. Cards work in malls, hotels and larger restaurants. ATMs at 7-Eleven and FamilyMart accept most international cards.

Tipping culture

  • Tipping is not expected in Taiwan.
  • Restaurants and hotels often add a 10% service charge to the bill.
  • Taxis: no tip. Rounding up the fare is welcome but optional.
  • Night markets and street stalls: no tip. A smile and a 謝謝 (xièxie) is plenty.

Power plugs

Taiwan uses 110V / 60Hz with Type A and Type B sockets (flat pins).

Bring an adapterIf your plugs have round pins, bring a flat-pin adapter. Most phone and laptop chargers accept 100–240V, so check the label. Hair dryers and curling irons may not work well at 110V.

Transportation

Taipei is easy without a car. The MRT goes almost everywhere, with buses and taxis filling the gaps.

Airport to the city

Taoyuan Airport (TPE) is about 40 km from central Taipei. Check your terminal (T1 or T2) on your ticket.

Airport MRT, step by step

  • Follow the purple "MRT" signs. Buy a token or tap an EasyCard.
  • Take the Express, not the slower Commuter train.
  • Get off at A1 Taipei Main Station. Changing to other MRT lines means a 10–15 minute walk underground.

Getting around Taipei

MRT (metro)

from NT$20about 06:00–24:00

Clean, frequent, with English signs. Lines are named by color and station code (R = Red). No eating or drinking in stations or trains. Stand on the right of escalators.

Bus

from NT$15

Pay with EasyCard. On most routes, tap when you board and again when you get off. Needed for the National Palace Museum and Yangmingshan. Google Maps shows live times.

YouBike

about NT$10–30 per hour

Rental bikes: pick up and return at any dock. Register through the YouBike 2.0 app with a credit card. Good for short, flat hops; skip it in rain or on mountain roads.

Taxi / Uber

base fare NT$85

Yellow taxis use a meter and can be waved down. The Uber app also works. Show the driver your destination in Chinese. Short city rides are about NT$100–250.

High-speed rail (HSR)

Taipei–Taichung about 1 hr

Useful for day trips to other cities, not needed for this itinerary. Taipei–Taichung is about NT$700. Stations: Taipei Main and Taoyuan (A18 on the Airport MRT).

EasyCard (悠遊卡)

about NT$100, non-refundable20% off MRT

A tap-to-pay card for MRT, buses, YouBike and convenience stores. Buy and top up at any MRT station or 7-Eleven. Start with NT$300–500.

Early departure: Dec 28, 09:10

Yangmingshan (陽明山)

Park freeHalf a day

Volcanic hills, hot-spring valleys and misty trails just north of the city.

Getting there: MRT Red Line to Jiantan, then Bus 260 (about 40 minutes).

Tip: Cold, windy and often foggy in December. Bring a coat and check the forecast; swap days if it is pouring.

Dihua Street (迪化街)

Free1.5–2 hours

Taipei's oldest trading street: Qing-era shophouses selling tea, dried seafood, herbs, fabric and snacks.

Getting there: MRT Orange Line to Daqiaotou, or bus.

Tip: A great place for souvenirs such as tea and dried fruit. Many shops open mid-morning.

Open in Google Maps

Jiufen (九份)

Free to wanderHalf a day

A hillside old mining town with steep lantern-lit alleys, tea houses and snacks, and views of the sea.

Getting there: Bus 1062 from MRT Zhongxiao Fuxing, Exit 2 (about NT$90, 1–1.5 hours, every 15–30 minutes). Or train to Ruifang, then a short bus.

Tip: Buses get very crowded. Go in the afternoon to see the lanterns at dusk, and bring a rain jacket. Weekdays are quieter.

Night markets

Shilin Night Market (士林夜市)

Free entry

Taipei's largest. Fried chicken cutlets, big sausages, oyster omelet and a food court in the basement.

Getting there: MRT Red Line to Jiantan, Exit 1 (a few minutes on foot). Open from about 16:00 to midnight.

Raohe Street Night Market (饒河街觀光夜市)

Free entry

One long street with a temple at the end. Try the pepper buns and herbal pork rib soup.

Getting there: MRT Green Line to Songshan, Exit 5. Open about 17:00–24:00. Start at the Songshan end.

Ningxia Night Market (寧夏夜市)

Free entry

A compact, food-focused market in the old Datong district: taro balls, oyster omelet, fried chicken, braised dishes.

Getting there: MRT Red Line to Shuanglian, about 10 minutes on foot. Open from about 17:00. Go early for short lines.

Open in Google Maps

Night market culture

  • Night markets are a Taiwanese way of life: dinner, snacks and games in one place, usually from late afternoon until midnight.
  • Cash only at most stalls. Bring small bills and coins.
  • Most dishes cost NT$50–100, so share and try many small plates.
  • A long line usually means it is good.
  • Look for the stall's signature item on the banner.
  • Bins are rare. Eat near the stall and take your trash with you.

Food Guide

Taiwan is a food lover's paradise. Eat often, share plates and try everything.

Food culture

  • Eating is the national hobby. Meals are casual, quick and shared, and often cost under NT$200.
  • Convenience stores are everywhere and sell good hot food.
  • Many places hand you a paper menu to tick and give to staff.
